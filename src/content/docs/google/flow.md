# Flow

## Overview
Flow is Google's AI-powered creative studio for filmmaking — generate, edit, and assemble video and image content from natural-language prompts, backed by Gemini, Veo, and Imagen models.

## What is it?
A web-based tool built around three stages: Plan (a Gemini-based creative agent that helps you explore and iterate on ideas), Create (generate video/images from text using Gemini Omni, Nano Banana, and Veo 3.1), and Refine (natural-language and professional editing controls). It also lets users build and share custom mini-tools for specific workflows (e.g., animated text overlays, storyboarding, video resizing).

## Why use it?
- Goes beyond a single text-to-video generation — supports planning, iterating, and refining a short film end-to-end in one tool.
- Veo 3.1 is positioned for strong physics/realism/prompt adherence; useful for more filmic output than typical short clips.
- Custom tool-building means teams can tailor Flow to a repeatable workflow rather than starting from scratch each time.

## Installation (how to access)
Web-based at [labs.google/fx/tools/flow](https://labs.google/fx/tools/flow). No install; requires a Google account and is restricted to users 18+. Regional availability varies. Free tier available; paid tiers unlock more generation credits.

## Basic Usage
1. Open Flow and start a project — describe your idea in the Plan stage.
2. Use Create to generate video/image clips from natural-language prompts with your chosen model (Gemini Omni, Nano Banana, Veo 3.1).
3. Use Refine to make edits via natural language or manual controls.
4. Assemble clips/tools into a finished short, or build/share a custom tool for a recurring task.

## Key Features
- Plan / Create / Refine workflow structure
- Multiple generative models: Gemini Omni (video from varied inputs), Nano Banana (image gen with subject/text consistency), Veo 3.1 (video, physics/realism-focused)
- Natural-language editing plus professional controls
- Custom, shareable creative tools (storyboarding, text overlays, resizing, VFX)
- Credit-based usage across free and paid tiers

## Top 5 Use Cases
1. Short-form AI-generated films or scenes
2. Storyboarding and previz before a real shoot
3. Marketing/social video content generation
4. Rapid concept videos for pitches
5. Building custom internal tools for a studio's repeatable video workflow

## Competitors
- Runway (Gen-3/4 video generation and editing)
- OpenAI Sora
- Pika Labs
- Adobe Premiere + Firefly video (traditional NLE with AI assist)

## Pros
- Combines multiple strong Google models (Veo 3.1, Imagen-family, Gemini) in one workflow
- Structured Plan/Create/Refine flow suits actual filmmaking, not just single clips
- Free tier available to try before paying

## Cons
- Paid tiers get expensive fast at scale ($99.99–$199.99/mo for Ultra) and generation costs vary by model/output
- 18+ and region-gated
- As a Labs product line, feature set and pricing are still evolving
- Video generation quality/consistency limits (typical of the category — artifacts, prompt drift on longer sequences)
